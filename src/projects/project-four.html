Drawing Machine Design

Mechanical design project

Summary: Designed and built a motor-driven drawing machine that uses purely mechanical motion to draw on a t-shirt. A chain of three linked axles, gears and sprockets converts one continuous motor rotation into layered rotating and orbiting motion at the pen tip, producing spirograph-like rosettes and looping flower patterns.

Skills

  • Mechanical design: axle, gear and sprocket layout, offsets and link lengths
  • Fabrication: cutting, drilling, assembly and alignment of a wooden frame and shafts
  • Kinematics, tolerances and structural rigidity

Technical details

How it works. A single motor spins a main axle. Attached to it is a second, offset axle driven through a gear and sprocket, so it both spins and orbits the center. The second axle drives a third, outer axle in the same way, and a pen is mounted at the end of that final link above a flattened t-shirt. Because every stage both rotates and revolves, the pen traces coupled rotations with different radii and offsets, which creates the layered rosettes and drifting loops.

Design. I sketched concepts for how chained axles, gears and sprockets could move a pen along non-trivial paths, then designed the layout of the three axles so each stage both spins and revolves around the previous one.

Build. I built a wooden base with three outward-slanting legs to form a rigid tripod-style structure, then mounted the motor, axles, gears and pen holder, aligning the parts to minimize friction and binding.

Testing. I ran drawing tests and observed how the patterns evolved as the mechanism cycled.

Results. The finished machine produced a wide range of geometric and organic patterns from one motor and a chain of mechanical linkages. The pen traced smooth, continuous curves (rosettes, flower-like shapes and nested loops) that slowly filled the page. Building it strengthened my understanding of kinematics, tolerances and structural rigidity. I saw firsthand how slight misalignment or flex in the frame affects motion quality and line smoothness.

Drawing Machine Design

Objectives

Design and build a motor-driven drawing machine that uses purely mechanical motion to draw a design on a t-shirt

Outcomes & Contributions

  • • Successfully designed and fabricated a motorized, three-link drawing machine mounted on a wooden panel with three outward-slanting legs
  • • Built a chained axle–gear–sprocket mechanism that transforms one continuous motor rotation into layered circular motions at the pen tip
  • • Demonstrated a family of spirograph-like patterns

Technical Details & Skills

  • • Mechanical design: Layout of axles, gears, and sprockets → choosing offsets and link lengths to create interesting pen paths
  • • Fabrication: Cutting and drilling the wooden base and tripod legs, assembling shafts, hubs, and pen mount, and aligning rotating components
  • • Basic electronics: Using a single motor and simple power control to drive the system continuously

Overview

This drawing machine is a small, harmonograph-style device that turns one motor's rotation into a beautiful looping flower artwork. A single motor spins a main axle. Rigidly attached to this axle is a second, offset axle driven through a gear and sprocket, so it both spins and orbits around the center. That motion is repeated again: the second axle drives a third, outer axle, and a pen is mounted at the end of this final link above a flattened out t-shirt.

Because each stage both rotates and revolves, the pen tip doesn't trace a simple circle. Instead, it follows a path shaped by several coupled rotations with different radii and offsets, producing layered rosettes, flower-like curves, and drifting loops over time. The entire mechanism is mounted on a wooden panel supported by three legs that slant outward, creating a stable tripod base so the machine can run without wobbling as it draws.

Work

The project focused on designing and building a simple but expressive mechanical drawing system from scratch. My work ranged from early sketches to the final product:

  • • Sketched concepts for how chained axles, gears, and sprockets could combine to move a pen in nontrivial paths
  • • Designed the layout of the main axle and two subsequent axles so each stage both spun and revolved around the previous one
  • • Built a wooden base and three outward-slanting legs to form a rigid, tripod-style structure
  • • Mounted the motor, axles, gears, and pen holder, paying attention to alignment so friction and binding would be minimized
  • • Ran drawing tests on poster paper, observing how the patterns evolved over time as the mechanism cycled

Results & Impact

The completed device produced a variety of geometric and organic patterns using only one motor and a chain of mechanical linkages. The pen traced out smooth, continuous curves (rosettes, flower-like shapes, and nested loops) that slowly filled the page as the axles rotated and revolved.

Building this drawing machine strengthened my understanding of kinematics, tolerances, and structural rigidity. I saw firsthand how slight misalignments or flexibility in the frame can influence motion quality and line smoothness. Overall, the project gave me a deeper, hands-on feel for how simple mechanical systems can create surprisingly complex and aesthetically pleasing behavior.
